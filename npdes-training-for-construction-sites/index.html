## THE SHORT VERSION
NPDES (National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System) is the Clean Water Act Section
402 permitting program that requires construction sites disturbing soil to control and
document their stormwater discharges. Multiple roles on a project may need
NPDES-related training depending on what the applicable permit requires of them.

Regulatory Basics

NPDES Training for Construction Sites

NPDES is the legal framework behind every construction stormwater permit in the country. Here's what it requires, who on a construction project actually needs NPDES-related training, and how this site's courses map to that need.

What NPDES Is and Why It Requires Training

The National Pollutant Discharge Elimination System (NPDES) is the permitting program established under Clean Water Act Section 402 to regulate discharges of pollutants into U.S. waters, including stormwater runoff from construction sites. Because construction activity disturbs soil and creates runoff, most construction projects above a certain size need an NPDES permit (typically a Construction General Permit) before breaking ground. That permit typically requires a written SWPPP, routine inspections, and documentation, all of which require trained people to execute correctly. See EPA's NPDES overview and stormwater discharges from construction activities pages.

Roles

Who Needs NPDES-Related Training

Permittees / Operators

The entity holding the NPDES/CGP permit, often the general contractor or project owner , is legally responsible for SWPPP compliance, even when day-to-day work is delegated to trained staff or consultants. Understanding NPDES requirements at a program level is essential for this role.

Contractors and Subcontractors

Site personnel may need training in inspections, BMP installation and maintenance, or documentation depending on their specific role and what their employer or the permittee requires of them.

SWPPP Preparers

Anyone drafting or revising the SWPPP needs to understand the NPDES/CGP requirements the plan must satisfy in order to write a compliant document.

Site Inspectors

Whoever performs routine and rain-event inspections needs to know what the applicable NPDES permit requires them to check and document.

How stormwaterplanning.us's Courses Map to NPDES Needs

Qualified SWPPP Practitioner (QSP) Training covers field implementation and inspection, installing and maintaining BMPs, conducting routine and rain-event inspections. Qualified SWPPP Preparer Training covers writing and revising the SWPPP document. Both are 7-hour, self-paced, English/Spanish courses priced at $379.99, and both issue a certificate of completion covering NPDES/CGP-aligned subject matter. NPDES itself does not certify individuals, it authorizes permits and, in most states, delegates permitting authority to a state program. A certificate from this site documents training content; it is not an NPDES certification and does not by itself satisfy every permit's specific training requirement.
