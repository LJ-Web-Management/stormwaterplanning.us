## CALIFORNIA VS. FEDERAL
California administers its own delegated NPDES Construction General Permit with:
- State-specific QSP/QSD qualification (CASQA / State Water Resources Control Board),
  separate from EPA's federal "qualified person" standard
- Numeric Action Levels (NALs) and Numeric Effluent Limits (NELs), pollutant concentration
  thresholds not present in the federal baseline permit
- The same general 1-acre disturbance trigger as the federal framework, with the same
  common-plan-of-development rules

California's SWPPP Framework

California implements its own delegated NPDES Construction General Permit rather than relying directly on EPA's federal permit, with additional state-specific elements: Numeric Action Levels (NALs) and Numeric Effluent Limits (NELs), and the formal QSP/QSD qualification system administered through CASQA and the State Water Resources Control Board. Any training provider operating in California, including this site, needs to be clear about which parts of that system its courses do and don't cover.

Choose Your Role

Do You Need a QSP or a QSD?

QSP, Qualified SWPPP Practitioner

For site supervisors, inspectors, and field crews implementing and inspecting the SWPPP: BMP installation and maintenance, required inspections, and site documentation. Our QSP Practitioner Training covers this subject matter area.

QSD, Qualified SWPPP Developer

For engineers, consultants, and compliance managers who write and revise the SWPPP document itself: risk assessment, BMP design criteria, and monitoring plans. Our SWPPP Preparer Training covers this subject matter area.

Not sure which one applies? See the full California QSP vs. QSD comparison, or Federal Qualified Person vs. California QSP/QSD for how California's system differs from the federal standard used in most other states.

Thresholds & Rules

California SWPPP Requirements

Disturbance Threshold

California generally follows the same one-acre disturbance trigger and common-plan-of-development rule as the federal baseline. See When Is a SWPPP Required? for how that threshold works.

Numeric Action Levels & Effluent Limits

California's Construction General Permit adds NALs and NELs, specific pollutant concentration thresholds not found in most other states' baseline permits. Exceeding an NAL typically triggers additional BMPs or reporting; exceeding an NEL can trigger stricter corrective action.

Risk-Based Permit Levels

California assigns projects a risk level (based on soil erosion potential and receiving water risk) that determines inspection frequency, monitoring requirements, and whether a QSD is required to sign the SWPPP.

Getting Certified in California

If your permit calls for California's formal QSP or QSD qualification, that credential is only granted through CASQA and the State Water Resources Control Board, not through this site or any general training provider.
