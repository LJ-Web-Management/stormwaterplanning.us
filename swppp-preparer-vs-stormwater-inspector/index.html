## THE SHORT VERSION
SWPPP Preparer is a document-authorship role: writing, organizing, and revising the
Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan. Stormwater Inspector is a field-inspection role:
performing routine and rain-event site inspections. The two roles are frequently held
by different people, though smaller projects sometimes combine them. "Stormwater
Inspector" is a general/functional title in most places, but some states run formal
inspector credentials, such as Washington and Oregon's CESCL (Certified Erosion and
Sediment Control Lead).

## KEY DISTINCTION
"SWPPP Preparer" is not a single nationally standardized credential, it describes a
function anyone can perform if their employer or permit requires them to be trained for
it. "Stormwater Inspector" is also mostly functional, EXCEPT in states that have created
a specific formal credential (e.g. CESCL in Washington and Oregon). Always confirm your
state and local permit's specific requirements.

Role Comparison

SWPPP Preparer vs. Stormwater Inspector

One role writes the plan. The other checks that the plan is being followed on the ground. Here's how the SWPPP Preparer and Stormwater Inspector roles differ, and where state-specific inspector credentials come into play.

Document Authorship vs. Field Inspection

A SWPPP Preparer writes, organizes, and revises the Stormwater Pollution Prevention Plan , the document describing a site's conditions, controls, and procedures. A Stormwater Inspector is the person who visits the site, routinely and after qualifying rain events , to check that the plan's controls are actually installed and functioning, and to document what they find. On smaller projects, one person may do both jobs; on larger projects, they're often separate roles held by different people.

Side by Side

How Each Role Works

SWPPP Preparer

A functional, document-authorship role rather than a single nationally standardized credential. The SWPPP Preparer writes and revises the SWPPP based on site conditions, applicable permit requirements, and planned construction activities. In California, the closest formal state equivalent is the QSD (Qualified SWPPP Developer) role. See Qualified SWPPP Preparer Training.

Stormwater Inspector

Also largely a functional role, responsible for routine and rain-event site inspections , but some states have created formal, state-specific inspector credentials. Washington and Oregon, for example, use the Certified Erosion and Sediment Control Lead (CESCL) credential. In California, the closest formal state equivalent is the QSP (Qualified SWPPP Practitioner) role. See Construction Stormwater Inspection Training.
