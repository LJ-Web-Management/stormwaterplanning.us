## THE SHORT VERSION
"Qualified person" is EPA's federal inspection-standard term under the 2022 Construction
General Permit (CGP). EPA does not certify individuals for this role. California's QSP
(Qualified SWPPP Practitioner) is a separate, formal state credential administered by
CASQA and the State Water Resources Control Board, requiring instruction, exam, renewal,
and registration.

| Renewal required        | N/A                                           | Yes, periodic                               |
| Registration            | N/A, permittee determines/documents qualification | Yes, registered/tracked through CASQA |
| Free training available | Yes, EPA Construction Inspection Training Course | No, CASQA course has a fee |
| Applies where            | Sites under EPA's directly-issued CGP (MA, NH, NM, DC, most territories/tribal lands) or as a general federal baseline concept | Sites under California's state CGP |

## KEY DISTINCTION
A "qualified person" determination under the federal CGP is left to the permittee/operator
, there is no EPA exam, renewal, or registry. California's QSP is a formally administered,
exam-based, registered credential. The two are not interchangeable, and a general online
training certificate (including from this site) satisfies neither by itself.

Federal vs. State

EPA "Qualified Person" vs. California QSP

EPA's federal Construction General Permit uses the term "qualified person" for site inspections, with no formal EPA certification process. California's QSP is a separate, formally administered state credential. Here's exactly how the two differ.

A Federal Standard vs. a State Credential

"Qualified person" and "QSP" are often used as if they describe the same thing, but they come from two different regulatory systems with two very different levels of formality. Understanding the difference matters for anyone deciding what training their project actually requires.

EPA does not certify, license, or credential individuals as a "qualified person." California's QSP, by contrast, is a formally administered credential with an exam, renewal, and registration requirement.

How Each Works

Federal "Qualified Person" and California QSP

EPA's Federal "Qualified Person" (CGP)

EPA's 2022 Construction General Permit (CGP) requires that site inspections be performed by a "qualified person", someone knowledgeable in the principles and practices of erosion and sediment control. EPA does not itself certify, license, or credential individuals for this role. EPA provides a free Construction Inspection Training Course as a resource, and it is the permittee/operator's responsibility to determine and document who on their team meets the standard. See EPA's Construction General Permit Inspector Training page.

California's QSP (Qualified SWPPP Practitioner)

California's statewide Construction General Permit requires a QSP to implement and inspect the SWPPP in the field. QSP status is administered through CASQA and the California State Water Resources Control Board, and requires prerequisites, Trainer of Record-led instruction, a proctored exam, periodic renewal, and formal registration.

Before You Enroll

Which One Applies to Your Project?

If your project is under EPA's directly-issued CGP (currently Massachusetts, New Hampshire, New Mexico, DC, and most territories and tribal lands) or under a state permit that uses the federal "qualified person" concept without a separate state credential, EPA's free training course and your employer's documented qualification process apply. If your project is under California's statewide Construction General Permit, you need the formally registered QSP credential through CASQA, a general training certificate does not substitute for it.
