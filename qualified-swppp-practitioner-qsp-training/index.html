Curriculum

What This Course Covers

Seven focused modules plus a final examination, built around real construction-site scenarios.

  1. 01

    SWPPP Fundamentals & Regulatory Framework

    Structure and goal of a SWPPP, and the Clean Water Act / NPDES permitting system it operates under.

  2. 02

    Role of the Qualified SWPPP Practitioner

    Professional duties and responsibilities of a QSP during active construction activities.

  3. 03

    Recognizing Stormwater Hazards

    Pollution discharges, sediment transport, and erosion risks common on construction sites.

  4. 04

    BMP Selection & Installation

    Choosing, installing, and maintaining the correct Best Management Practices for site conditions.

  5. 05

    Site Inspections & Reporting

    Performing inspections, documenting findings, and recommending corrective actions on deficiencies.

  6. 06

    Pollution Prevention in the Field

    Safe work practices near storm drains and water bodies, chemical storage, waste management, and spill response.

  7. 07

    Site Conditions & Best Professional Judgment

    Interpreting site maps, soil, and weather to forecast problems, and coordinating with owners, contractors, and designers.

  8. 08

    Final Examination

    A final examination is required to complete the course and receive a certificate of completion.

Credential Transparency & State-Specific Notes

stormwaterplanning.us does not claim EPA approval, EPA endorsement, NPDES certification, or California QSP/QSD certification for this course. A certificate of completion documents that training content was completed, it is not a government-issued credential.

Permit Operator Responsibility

Permit operators remain responsible for ensuring that stormwater team members meet the training requirements of the applicable federal, state, or local permit.

State-Specific Limitations

This training is based on federal EPA regulations under the Clean Water Act (CWA) Section 402 and 40 CFR 122.26, which apply nationwide. Many states administer their own NPDES programs and may require additional state-specific training. In states where the EPA remains the permitting authority (Massachusetts, New Hampshire, New Mexico, D.C. and most U.S. territories/tribal lands), training based on federal EPA permit requirements is generally sufficient; other states may require more.

A Note on QSP Terminology

QSP terminology may have state-specific meanings. In California, QSP/QSD qualification is administered through the CASQA/State Water Board process and may require prerequisites, Trainer of Record instruction, exams, renewal, and registration. This course provides stormwater practitioner training and a certificate of completion. See Federal vs. California QSP/QSD for details. This course is also listed as SWPPP Practitioner Field Inspection Training.
