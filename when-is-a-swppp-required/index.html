## THE SHORT ANSWER
Under EPA's federal Construction General Permit (CGP) framework, a SWPPP is required once
a project disturbs one acre or more of land, or is part of a larger common plan of
development that will ultimately disturb one acre or more. States and some local
jurisdictions can set lower thresholds or add requirements on top of the federal baseline,
so the applicable permit, not a general rule of thumb, controls.

## KEY TRIGGERS
1. Disturbed acreage reaches 1 acre or more (federal baseline, 40 CFR 122.26)
2. Project is phased but part of a common plan of development totaling 1 acre or more
3. State or local rule sets a threshold below 1 acre (varies by state)
4. Discharge is to an MS4 in a jurisdiction with its own local trigger
5. Project is in or near an impaired water body with additional permit conditions

## SWPPP VS. PERMIT
The permit (NPDES / state Construction General Permit) is the legal authorization to
discharge stormwater. The SWPPP is the site-specific plan document required as a
condition of holding that permit. Permit coverage comes first; the SWPPP is what the
permit requires you to prepare, implement, and keep current.

Permits & Requirements

When Is a SWPPP Required?

The short answer: once a project disturbs one acre or more of land under EPA's federal framework. The complete answer depends on your state, your permit, and how your project is phased. Here's how to tell where your project stands.

The Quick Answer

A SWPPP is required once a construction project disturbs one acre or more of soil, under EPA's NPDES Construction General Permit (CGP) framework (40 CFR 122.26). Projects under one acre can still need a SWPPP if they're part of a larger common plan of development that totals one acre or more, and many states and some municipalities set their own, often lower, thresholds. The permit that actually applies to your site, not a general rule of thumb, is what decides it.

Federal Baseline

The One-Acre Threshold, Explained

What Counts as "Disturbed" Land

Clearing, grading, excavating, demolition, or stockpiling that exposes soil all count toward disturbed acreage, not just the building footprint. A one-acre site with a small building pad can still trigger SWPPP requirements once access roads, staging areas, and utility trenching are included.

What Counts as a "Common Plan of Development"

If a project is phased or sold off in parcels, but the site was planned, permitted, or sold as part of a larger development, EPA generally treats the whole plan's total disturbed acreage as the trigger, not each phase in isolation. A 0.75-acre phase inside a 5-acre subdivision still needs SWPPP coverage.

Smaller Than One Acre? Check Local Rules

Some states and municipalities apply construction stormwater requirements below the federal one-acre threshold, particularly in MS4 (municipal separate storm sewer system) jurisdictions or near impaired waters. A sub-acre project can be exempt federally and still require local sign-off.

Two Different Things

A SWPPP Is Not the Same as a Permit

"Swppp permit" is a common search, but a SWPPP and a permit are two different items that work together. The permit, typically an NPDES or state Construction General Permit (CGP), is the legal authorization to discharge stormwater from the site. The SWPPP is the site-specific plan document that the permit requires you to prepare, implement, and keep current, covering site conditions, drainage, BMPs, inspections, and corrective actions. You obtain permit coverage first (often through a Notice of Intent), and the SWPPP is one of the ongoing obligations that coverage requires. See How to Prepare a SWPPP for what actually goes into the document itself.

It Depends on Where You're Building

State & Local Rules Can Change the Threshold

State NPDES Programs

Most states administer their own delegated NPDES Construction General Permit rather than EPA's direct federal permit, and state programs can add stricter thresholds, additional inspection intervals, or numeric discharge limits on top of the federal baseline. See State Stormwater Permit Requirements for a state-by-state starting point.

California's SWPPP Requirements

California runs one of the more detailed state Construction General Permits, with its own Numeric Action Levels, Numeric Effluent Limits, and the state-specific QSP/QSD qualification system for who can prepare and implement a SWPPP. See California SWPPP, QSP & QSD Training for how California's rules differ from the federal framework.

MS4 & Local Ordinances

Cities and counties operating a Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4) permit can layer local construction stormwater ordinances on top of state and federal rules, sometimes with lower acreage thresholds or additional inlet-protection and erosion-control standards.
